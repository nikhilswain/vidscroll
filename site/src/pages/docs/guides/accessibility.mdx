A scroll video is driven entirely by the reader: nothing moves unless they
scroll. That avoids the biggest problem with motion on the web, but a few
things still need care.

## What vidscroll handles

- **Reduced motion.** `smoothScroll` turns itself off when the visitor has
  asked their system for reduced motion, so the page scrolls normally.
- **Keyboard scrolling.** Arrow keys, Page Down and Space scroll the page and
  the video as usual. Smooth scrolling never interferes with them.
- **Real text.** Section content is ordinary HTML, not part of the video, so
  it can be read by screen readers, translated, selected and zoomed.
- **No sound.** Scroll-driven video is always muted, so there's no audio to
  caption.

## What to keep in mind

### Inactive sections are hidden

Sections are hidden while inactive, from screen readers too. A screen reader
user scrolling with the keyboard hears each section as it becomes active, but
can't read ahead. If a section holds information people need, such as a key
fact or a link, also put it somewhere that's always available, for example in
the page content after the video.

### The video carries no meaning on its own

The video itself has no text alternative. Make sure the story is told by the
text in your sections and the page around the video, so someone who can't see
the video doesn't miss anything important.

### Contrast over moving pictures

Text over video changes contrast as the footage changes. Give captions a
background, a strong shadow, or place them over a darker part of the frame.
Check them against the brightest frames of the video, not just the first one.

### Buttons over the video

Content outside a section ignores clicks by default; interactive elements need
`pointer-events: auto`. Buttons inside a section can only be reached by
keyboard while that section is active. Put controls that should always work,
such as chapter buttons, outside any section.

### Very long scroll videos

A long `length` means a lot of scrolling for keyboard and switch users. Keep
videos short, and add a way to skip past, such as a link to the content after
the video:

```tsx title="Hero.tsx"
<a href="#after-video" className="skip-link">Skip the video</a>
<ScrollVideo src="/videos/hero.mp4" length="400vh" />
<section id="after-video">…</section>
```

<Callout title="Flashing">
  Avoid footage with rapid flashes or strong flicker. Scrubbing quickly can
  make flashes faster than in normal playback.
</Callout>
