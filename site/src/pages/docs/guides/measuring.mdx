A scroll video can feel smooth on a fast laptop and stutter on a phone. Most
visitors are on phones, so test on one, and measure rather than judge by eye.

## The stats panel in the demos

The demos have a built-in readout. Open a demo with `?stats` before the `#` in
its address, on the device you want to test, and scroll:

```text
https://nikhilswain.github.io/vidscroll/demos/?stats#/commute
```

<Table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Reading</th><th>What it tells you</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Frames shown per second while scrolling</td><td>How smooth the video looks. 30 or more reads as smooth</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Jump between shown frames</td><td>How many video frames are skipped between two shown frames. Small is good</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Seek time</td><td>How long the browser takes to show a requested frame</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Page frame rate and long frames</td><td>Whether the rest of the page keeps up. Frames over 50 ms are visible hitches</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Usable after, re-encode</td><td>How long until scrolling works, and how long an in-browser re-encode took</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</Table>

**Copy report** puts all of it, plus the device details, on the clipboard.
Compare reports before and after a change.

<Callout title="Use HTTPS on phones">
  In-browser re-encoding and caching only work on secure pages. A phone loading
  your dev server over plain HTTP on the local network can't use them, so its
  results won't match production. Test on an HTTPS deployment, or use the
  demo site.
</Callout>

## On your own page

- **The console.** vidscroll logs a warning for anything that hurts scrubbing:
  sparse keyframes, a re-encode, a large or long file, a failed CORS read. No
  warnings is the goal.
- **`onLoad`.** Its `source` tells you how the video was served: `"original"`
  (the file was fine as it is), `"optimized"` (re-encoded this visit), `"cache"`
  (re-encoded on an earlier visit) or `"stream"` (couldn't be read).
- **Remote debugging.** On Android, connect the phone over USB and open
  `chrome://inspect` in desktop Chrome to see the phone's console and record a
  performance profile while you scroll. Safari offers the same for iPhones
  through its Develop menu.
- **`debug`.** The `debug` prop logs the engine's internals to the console,
  which is mostly useful when reporting a problem.

## What usually helps

<Table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Symptom</th><th>Try</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Low frames shown per second, slow seeks</td><td>Prepare the file with <Link href="/docs/videos/cli/">the CLI</Link>, or lower its resolution</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Low page frame rate, many long frames</td><td>Simplify the effects on top: avoid animated blur and large shadows</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Long wait before scrolling works</td><td>A smaller file, or a prepared one so no re-encode runs</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Big jumps while flicking fast</td><td>Normal at very high speeds; <code>smoothScroll</code> evens out wheel scrolling</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</Table>
